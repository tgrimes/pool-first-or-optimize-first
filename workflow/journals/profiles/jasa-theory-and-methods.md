## Scope

> "The Theory and Methods section publishes articles that make original contributions to the foundations, theoretical development, and methodology of statistics and probability." [17]

> "Articles focus on statistical applications, theory, and methods throughout all disciplines that make use of data, including economic, social, biological, physical, engineering, and health sciences and the humanities." [18]

In plain terms, this is the ASA's flagship journal, which the ASA calls "the premier journal of statistical science" [1], and Theory and Methods is the section for new statistical methodology and its theory; the readers are methodologists. JASA has two research sections, Theory and Methods and Applications and Case Studies, and a paper goes to only one: "Papers can be submitted for publication to only one section at a time." [17] The two sections share one journal, so they share the ASA style guide, the reproducibility review [3], [17], the Taylor & Francis AI policy, the open-access option, and the submission system; what differs is what the paper is for. The instructions warn that "more narrowly focused contributions, while of considerable merit in their own right, may be inappropriate for the Theory and Methods section" [17]. The group's critique papers fit when the closed-form result about what a procedure estimates is the main contribution and the genomics application illustrates it.

| Paper type | Fit | Why |
|--------------------------------------------|----------|------------------------------------------------|
| Critique with a closed-form bias, simulation, fix, and application | good | Original contributions to statistical methodology are the section's stated aim [17], and recent JASA papers derive what a common practice actually does (Rizzelli et al. 2026) and give inference for widely used genomics estimates (Cai et al. 2024). |
| Simulation and diagnostic, no closed form | poor | The section asks for original contributions to theory and methodology and warns that narrowly focused contributions may be inappropriate [17]; every recent JASA paper in this area found carries new theory or a new method. |
| Short methods note | poor | JASA's only short research format, the 25-page Short Communication, belongs to the Applications and Case Studies section [17]. |
| Theory paper | good | Foundations and theoretical development of statistics and probability are the section's stated aim [17]. |
| Applied analysis | poor | Applied work belongs in the Applications and Case Studies section, and a paper may be submitted to only one section at a time [17]. |

Formatting that is stated: submissions must be 12 point and fully double spaced, "which we define as 26 lines of text per page", and appendices of more than two paragraphs go into online supplements [2]. The instructions repeat the 12 point, 26 lines per page rule and add: "Appendices, proofs, and other supporting material should be placed in a separate supplement file (anonymized for review)." [17]

## Abstract

- Structured: no. The instructions ask for "an unstructured abstract of 200 words" with no references [17], and the ASA style guide asks the abstract to "summarize results, topics discussed, and main conclusions" with no displays, no complex mathematical notation, and no references, and names no headings [2].
- Headings: none.
- Word limit: 200 words [2], [17]. "The first 100 words should succinctly describe the paper's motivation and contribution." [17] The style guide adds: "If an abstract exceeds the word limit, it will be edited to meet the length restriction." [2] Key words follow the abstract; the instructions give two different counts, and the style guide says three to five (see Contradictions below) [2], [17].

## Reference style

- The journal's stated style: ASA author-year style. The instructions say references are "cited in text with the authors' names and dates of publication", point to the ASA Style Guide, and accept BibTeX with natbib and `plain.bst` or `apalike.bst`; the JASA LaTeX template is described as "compatible with Quarto and Overleaf" [17]. In the style guide, in-text citations give the author's surname and date ("Efron (2020)", or "(Lee 2008; Ludwig and Miller 2007)" with no comma between name and year); "et al." for three or more authors; the reference list is alphabetized, gives all authors (more than six may be cut to six plus "et al."), puts the year in parentheses followed by a comma, the article title in double quotation marks, the journal name spelled out in italics, volume, pages, and a DOI if available [2].
- CSL file: `dependent/journal-of-the-american-statistical-association.csl` in the official styles repository (github.com/citation-style-language/styles, commit d2cc420, 2026-10-24), a dependent style whose parent is `american-statistical-association.csl`.
- File to use in Quarto: `csl/american-statistical-association.csl`, which renders offline. The journal-named `csl/dependent/journal-of-the-american-statistical-association.csl` gives the same output but needs a network connection, because pandoc fetches the parent at render time.
- Rendering test: both files passed on 2026-10-27 (`csl/test-results.md`).

## Supplement, code, and data

- Supplementary material: appendices, proofs, and other supporting material go in a separate supplement file, anonymized for review [17]. The style guide adds that appendices longer than two paragraphs must be online supplements, cited as "the online Appendix" or "online Appendix A", without LaTeX cross-referencing to the main paper; references cited only in supplements get their own list there [2]. The ASA "prefers that code and data are submitted as supplemental material" [2]. The reproducibility guide asks for the Author Contributions Checklist as a separate PDF and the reproducibility materials as one zip file, suggested name `reproducibility_materials.zip` [3].
- Data availability statement: no data availability statement as such is named on the reachable pages. Instead, "authors are required to include an Author Contributions Checklist (ACC) Form with their submission", and the ACC form "should indicate where the code and data will be made publicly available upon publication of the manuscript" [4]. Taylor & Francis assigns each journal a data sharing tier stated in its Instructions for Authors [13]; JASA's tier, and any data availability statement, were not recorded from the instructions page [17].
- Code: required, with the first revision. The instructions say: "For new manuscripts submitted as of September 1, 2021, manuscripts being considered for publication in both the Applications & Case Studies and Theory & Methods sections of JASA undergo reproducibility review." Authors invited to revise are "required to submit the completed ACC Form along with relevant code and data together with their revised manuscript"; the materials are posted to Figshare, and identifying information should be removed from code where possible [17]. The reproducibility guide says materials have been required "for Theory and Methods submissions since 2021" [3]. Initial submissions: "We encourage but do not require that initial submissions include the code, data, and workflow." Invited revisions "must include code, data, and the workflow to reproduce the work presented" [3]. "Once a manuscript is conditionally accepted, the code and data (unless an exception is granted for the latter) must be made available as detailed in the ACC form", and code and data under 500 MB in the supplement are deposited on acceptance in the JASA area of Figshare [4]. A public repository is not required at submission: "code and data need not be publicly available at the time of submission when there are concerns about confidentiality" [4], and authors "might choose to keep the repository private until the time of publication" [3]. Confidential data can be replaced by a pseudo-dataset that runs with the code [4].
- Reproducibility review: one of the JASA associate editors for reproducibility (AER) "is asked to carry out this review" of the materials [3]; the ASA describes this code, data, and workflow evaluation as part of all invited revisions to JASA [5]. A GitHub template repository is offered (`jasa-acs/repro-template`) [3]. The about page lists 18 Associate Editors for Reproducibility [18].

## AI-use policy

> "Declaration of generative AI use. Please include a statement disclosing whether and how generative AI has been used in the research and manuscript preparation process." [17]

> "You must not list AI tools as a co-author of your article." "Please add a Declaration of generative AI use statement disclosing whether and how generative AI has been used." [6]
>
> "Taylor & Francis does not allow generative AI to create a first draft of a manuscript or sections of a manuscript." [7]

The first quotation is a checklist item on the JASA instructions page [17]; the others are publisher-wide policies: the authorship page says its instructions "apply to all Taylor & Francis Group journals" [6], and the images page says the same [8]. Paraphrase of the rest: AI may improve spelling, grammar, and flow, but "cannot be used to create or conclude the argument of an article" [7]; AI may generate software code if the author reviews and verifies it [7]; the statement must give the tool's name and version, how and why it was used, and confirm that the authors take full responsibility for the content, including the accuracy of references [6, 7]; AI use in research methods must also be detailed in the methods section [7]; AI must not be used to create or manipulate images and figures from research data, AI help with data visualisations is allowed, and any AI-generated image needs a statement in its figure legend [8]. If no AI was used, the suggested wording is "The authors report generative AI was not used in their research or preparation of this manuscript" [6]. Reviewers and editors must not upload unpublished manuscripts to AI tools [9].

- Read at: https://www.tandfonline.com/action/authorSubmission?show=instructions&journalCode=uasa20, https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/editorial-policies/defining-authorship-research-paper/, and https://authorservices.taylorandfrancis.com/editorial-policies/using-ai-in-your-research-and-manuscript-preparations/, 2026-10-27.
- What the manuscript must do: carry a "Declaration of generative AI use" statement naming each tool and version, saying how and why it was used (code, editing) and that the authors take full responsibility, repeat any methodological AI use in the Methods, and contain no AI-drafted text; where the statement goes is not stated on the instructions page [17] or the publisher pages, and the ASA style guide's section order (Acknowledgments, Funding, Disclosure Statement) has no slot for it [2]. The separate disclosure (conflict of interest) statement goes "at the end of your manuscript, after any acknowledgments and prior to the references" [17].

## Open access and charges

- Model: hybrid. The about page calls JASA "a hybrid open access journal that is part of the Taylor & Francis Open Select publishing program" [18]. The ASA style guide says JASA is copyrighted by the ASA and "Authors are able to retain copyright and make articles Open Access through payment of the Article Processing Charge (APC)" [2].
- Article processing charge: none unless the authors choose open access: "If you choose not to publish open access in this journal, there is no APC." [18] The open access charge is USD 4,320 for "Article - Theory & Methods", plus VAT or local taxes, with the country set to United States; read 2026-10-27 [11]. "There are no submission fees, publication fees or page charges for this journal." Colour in print costs $400 per figure [17].
- Waivers and institutional agreements: "You may be eligible for a reduced or fully-waivered APC if your institution has an agreement with Taylor & Francis." [10]

## Review

- Model: double anonymized. Papers are "double anonymous peer reviewed by expert referees", but "The associate editor is not anonymous." Authors submit an anonymous file and an identified file, and remove clues to their identity, including references to unpublished work and funding [17]. Separately, invited revisions get a reproducibility review by an associate editor for reproducibility [3], [17].
- Time to decision: "63 days avg. from submission to first decision"; 102 days on average from submission to the first post-review decision; 96 days on average from acceptance to online publication; acceptance rate 14% [18]. The about page gives these for the journal as a whole, not by section [18].

Practical note: the ScholarOne page says all new JASA submissions go through Taylor & Francis's new Submission Portal, and ScholarOne is kept only for revisions of submissions started there [14]; the instructions also send submissions to the Submission Portal [17].

### Contradictions

- Number of key words: the instructions ask for "between 3 and 5 keywords" in one place and "from three to six key words or phrases that do not appear in the paper's title" in another [17]; the ASA style guide says three to five [2].
- When the Author Contributions Checklist is due: the reproducibility guide says "authors are required to include an Author Contributions Checklist (ACC) Form with their submission" [4], while the instructions say authors invited to revise are sent ACC instructions and submit the form "together with their revised manuscript" [17].
